9.1

Commercial v. Government Plans

When discussing insurance with a caller it is important to recognize these two insurance types.

Commercial — privately funded

Primarily provided through employer-sponsored plans

  • HMO
  • PPO
  • POS
  • High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
  • Exclusive Provider Organization (EPO)
  • Private Fee-For-Service (PFFS)
Government — taxpayer funded

Financed by federal and state taxes

  • Medicare
  • Medicaid
  • Peachstate
  • Ambetter
  • Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP)
  • Wellcare
  • Federal/state employee health plans
  • Veterans Health Administration (VHA)
  • Tricare
Next step: once you have identified the insurance type, use the Insurance Lookup to confirm the physician's preference. If a plan is not accepted by the desired provider, patients may either proceed with scheduling as a self-pay patient or schedule with a different provider that does accept their health plan.
9.2

Insurance Types

If a caller is having a difficult time identifying their insurance type, direct them to look at the bottom corners of their card.

If the caller still struggles, default to PPO — the Central Business Office (CBO) will verify patient insurance information once it is in the system.
HMO

Health Maintenance Organization

Have one in-network PCP that provides routine care and refers you to in-network specialists. Referrals are required, and out-of-network care will not be covered.

EPO

Exclusive Provider Organization

Similar to HMO, only in-network care is covered. Generally have larger networks than HMO. Similar to PPO, no referrals required for specialist care.

POS

Point of Service

Have one in-network PCP that provides routine care and manages your access to other doctors. Referrals to specialists are required. You can also visit out-of-network physicians at a higher cost.

PPO

Preferred Provider Organization

Not required to have a PCP and do not need referrals to see specialists. You can choose which doctors you see. Out-of-network physicians will cost more.

9.3

Multiplan and Medi-Share

These are not traditional insurance carriers, so they're handled a bit differently from the rest of the Insurance Grid.

🌐 Multiplan and PHCS

Nationwide PPO provider network that allows you to choose which doctors you see. Multiplan and PHCS are not insurance plans, but broad networks of providers. Insurance companies can contract with these networks to access discounted services for their members.

✅ All our providers accept Multiplan.

🤝 Medi-Share

Healthcare sharing program that consists of a community sharing financial resources and paying each other's medical expenses. This is not an insurance plan, but a service a patient may have.

Only our Piedmont Providers and our Pediatric Providers accept MediShare.

9.4

Special Cases

In addition to the numerous plans our physicians accept in their office, there are also insurance cases that require you to receive and/or communicate extra information when scheduling.

💵 Self-Pay

The minimum out of pocket cost for an in-office Self Pay visit is $250.

That amount is just to cover the office visit with the provider. This means that if the doctor orders labs or tests the cost may increase (labs and tests are at the doctor's discretion at the time of the visit).

Remember: document in the appointment details that you have communicated this policy to the caller. It is important to share this information upfront to ensure there are no surprises at the time of the visit.

📋 Insurance with Referral Requirements

Depending upon the insurance policy, a referral may or may not be necessary for an appointment. Please confirm with the caller whether it is required by their insurance.

There are three exceptions to this procedure — in these cases the referral is mandatory:

Kaiser
Patient must bring/fax the referral and provide the referral number.
Tricare
Patient must bring/fax the referral.
Triwest (now Optum)
Patient must bring/fax the referral.

Patients can still schedule with us even if they do not have their referral on hand. Please encourage the patient to obtain the referral as you are scheduling the appointment.

9.4 (cont.)

Care Credit and Financial Assistance

💳 Care Credit

Care Credit is a form of payment for medical bills, not an insurance plan. Patients may or may not have an insurance policy in addition to having Care Credit.

Callers using Care Credit must be informed that their out-of-pocket expense must be at least $300 before our office can accept it as a form of payment.

Remember: add to the appointment detail that the patient has Care Credit and is aware of the $300 minimum.

🤝 Financial Assistance

Most medical facilities offer assistance programs to ease the financial burden of medical expenses for patients in need. Some of our offices do not accept these programs — you can clarify this information with the CBO or the office's front desk.

9.5

Insurance and Advanced Practitioners

As with physicians, our Physician Assistants (PA) and Nurse Practitioners (NP) have insurance guidelines we must adhere to so confusion can be avoided for our patients. Most advanced practitioners are paired with a specific physician in our practice.

Commercial insurance: determine whether an advanced practitioner will accept a patient's plan by referring to the corresponding physician's Insurance Grid in the Insurance Lookup.
Government-funded insurance: consult the practitioner's preference guideline for any stipulations.
9.6

Involving the Central Business Office

Callers can reach the Central Business Office (CBO) by the direct number, or by calling the Patient Support Center and then being transferred. You will need to reach out to that department whenever you receive a question about:

  • Balances and copays
  • Payment plans
  • Procedure costs
  • Insurance authorizations
  • Insurance codes
  • Collections
Giving financial advice, excluding self-pay consultation fees, is outside of the Patient Support Center's scope.

Cold transfer vs. warm transfer

Generally, you can cold transfer a patient to the CBO. However, pay attention to the nuances in each situation! If a caller has already expressed their unsuccessful attempts at speaking with a CBO representative, that is a great time to perform a warm transfer.

Just need to confirm an insurance?

If you need to confirm whether a physician will accept a caller's insurance, use the CBO as your resource — you do not need to transfer the patient in this case. Just have the name of the insurance plan and member ID ready to share with a CBO representative yourself. Someone will be able to give a definitive answer, and you would have cut out additional holding time for the caller!
